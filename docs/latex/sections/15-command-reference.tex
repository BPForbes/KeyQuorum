\section{Command reference}
\label{sec:command-reference}

The rest of this manual explains what each area of the CLI is for and how
the pieces fit together; this section is the complete index of every
\code{keyquorum} and \code{keyquorum-device} command and subcommand, so
this manual alone --- without a separate run of \code{--help} --- says
what the tool can do. Each row summarizes what \code{--help}
prints for that command. Flags are not reproduced here in full; run
\code{keyquorum <command> --help} (or \code{keyquorum <command>
<subcommand> --help}) for a command's complete flag list, defaults, and
which flags conflict with or require others.

\subsection{Identity and hardware keys}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\code{generate} & Generate an encryption or signing keypair. The private
  key prints to stdout once and is never written to disk by this tool;
  \flag{--register} also registers the new public key. \\
\code{register} & Register an existing public key under a label. \\
\code{list} & List registered hardware keys and live split trees. \\
\code{revoke} & Revoke a registered key: drops its pairings and
  private-bridge membership everywhere; \flag{--evict} also PSS-refreshes
  the surviving siblings of its leaf. \\
\code{remove} & Remove a registered hardware key. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Split-key trees}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\code{split} & Split a secret into a live tree. \flag{--leaf} builds the
  tree directly (siblings are bound automatically); \flag{--tree-spec} is
  for restoring a nested snapshot. \\
\code{bind} & Pair two nodes (establish \code{node <-> peer}), or reseal a
  leaf onto a new public key. \\
\code{add} & Insert a leaf under a parent split and reshare that parent. \\
\code{tree} & Print every live tree, one tree by id, the lowest common
  ancestor of \flag{--node} labels, or write the live spec as JSON. \\
\code{tree publish} & Upload a tree's public topology (no sealed shares) to
  the relay. \\
\code{tree restructure} & Announce a restructured tree: one authenticated
  envelope per active leaf at the next public generation. Unlike
  \code{publish}, recipients can verify who authorized the change. Signs with
  a key file or \flag{--slot}; \flag{--push} uploads the envelopes. \\
\code{tree countersign} & Countersign a pending restructure proposal (key
  file, or \flag{--device}/\flag{--slot}); \flag{--push} uploads. \\
\code{tree fetch} & Download the slice a pull key is allowed to see and
  merge it into this store. The key id is optional when the store holds one
  tree. \\
\code{reconstruct} & Reconstruct a key's secret from raw shares (key
  files, container slots, or an interactive prompt). \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Delivery and the mailbox relay}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\code{send} & Seal a file (or a tracked file's newest trusted revision) to a
  registered label, queue it in your outbox ring and send it from there: upload
  it, or write it to \file{./outbox}. \flag{--quorum-file} sends a quorum-protected file unlocked in memory. \\
\code{inbox} & Pull what is waiting for you from the relay and list it
  (\code{inbox list} is the same, spelled out).
  \code{inbox open} opens each letter and posts the signed answer, including
  bridge and organization updates;
  with a letter id it also takes \flag{--into}/\flag{--out} (a tracked file),
  \flag{--file} (the copy an acknowledgement, request answer or snapshot
  concerns) and \flag{--accept}/\flag{--decline} (a request). A delivered
  letter's slot is released and its file deleted; \code{inbox drop ID}
  discards one unopened. \\
\code{outbox} & Your outbox ring buffer of sealed letters (\file{.kqpb}) for
  trusted recipients: show it (index, size, state); \code{add LETTER... --to
  LABEL} queues at the write pointer (\flag{--file} names your copy when a
  tracked-file letter's order must be checked), \code{send} (\flag{--all},
  \flag{--output-dir}) sends from the read pointer, oldest first,
  \code{drop} discards the oldest, \code{capacity N} resizes an empty ring,
  \code{refusals} (\flag{--limit}) lists the letters turned away at
  departure and the rule each broke. \\
\code{deliver send} & (Legacy; use \code{send}.) Seal a file to a registered
  label, signed with your signing key. \\
\code{deliver open} & (Legacy; use \code{inbox open}.) Open a letter addressed to you: verify the sender,
  keep (or \flag{--reject}) the file, and seal a signed acknowledgement
  back. \\
\code{deliver ack} & (Legacy; use \code{inbox open}.) Check an acknowledgement sealed back to you. \\
\code{relay push} & Upload every \file{.kqpb} in a directory to the relay;
  also merges the public trees stored in \flag{--db} into the relay's documents
  (unrelated nodes stay; \code{tree publish} is the command that replaces one). \\
\code{relay pull} & (Legacy for letters; use \code{inbox}.) Download envelopes and the public-tree slice for a
  pull key; \flag{--import} installs each envelope into \flag{--db}
  directly. \\
\code{loadkey} & Check a relay API key with \code{POST /keycheck} and
  store it (and its hash) on this instance for later relay commands to
  reuse. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Legacy commands (still possible, not recommended)}
\label{sec:legacy-commands}

These commands keep working exactly as before: standard output and the exit
status are unchanged, and after one succeeds a note on standard error names the
command to use instead. They are not recommended for new use.
\code{relay push} is not legacy.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.34\linewidth}p{0.60\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Legacy command (prints a note)} & \textbf{Use instead} \\
\midrule
\code{deliver send} & \code{send FILE --to LABEL} \\
\code{deliver open} & \code{inbox open} \\
\code{deliver ack} & \code{inbox open} (it reads the answer) \\
\code{relay pull} (letters) & \code{inbox} to list, \code{inbox open} to open \\
\code{file share} & \code{send FILE.kqtf --to LABEL} \\
\code{file receive} & \code{inbox open ID --into FILE} or \flag{--out FILE} \\
\code{file ack} & \code{inbox open ID --file FILE} \\
\code{file verify-snapshot} & \code{file history verify SNAPSHOT} \\
\code{file history --export} & \code{file history export FILE --out SNAPSHOT} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection*{Older forms that print no note}

Four tracked-file commands are not marked legacy and print nothing, but
\code{inbox open} now does the same job when the letter came through a relay.
They remain the way to open a letter that reached you as a file.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.34\linewidth}p{0.60\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{With a relay} \\
\midrule
\code{file open-request}, \code{file answer-request} & \code{inbox open ID --accept} or \flag{--decline}, with \flag{--file FILE} \\
\code{file open-answer} & \code{inbox open ID --file FILE} \\
\code{file open-history} & \code{inbox open ID --file FILE} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent A letter that reaches you as a file (carried by hand, no relay) is
still opened with the \code{file} and \code{deliver} commands, because
\code{inbox} reads a relay. A producer command followed by \code{relay push}
also still works and prints no note; \flag{--push} on the producer command does
both in one step.

\subsection{Tracked files}

See section~\ref{sec:file-history}.

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\code{file track} & Start tracking a file: make its first revision, sign it as its author, and write \file{<file>.kqtf}. \flag{--no-auto-merge} sends every fork to a person. \flag{--owner-rule}, \flag{--descendants-rule}, \flag{--ancestors-rule} and \flag{--cross-branch-rule} set what each role must present (\code{forbidden}, \code{author}, \code{author+parent}, or for cross-branch \code{author+bridge-or-owner} / \code{author+bridge+owner}); rules that cannot be met are refused. \\
\code{file policy} & Show the rules a tracked file's revisions are judged by and the policy hash every revision carries. \\
\code{file checkin} & Check in a new revision from a native file (signed with \flag{--slot}, or \flag{--unsigned}). \\
\code{file sign} & Sign a revision you authored (the current head unless \flag{--revision}). Given a native file that is not tracked, this first content signature starts tracking it, exactly as \code{file track} does; the scope is your own label unless \flag{--scope} names another. \\
\code{file rename} & Change the name a tracked file is shown under. The scope owner or an ancestor proves the label with \flag{--slot}; the file id and every revision id stay the same and the old and new names are recorded. \\
\code{file countersign} & Countersign a revision the author has signed. \\
\code{file finalize} & Mark a trusted revision final (scope owner or an ancestor, by slot). Trust itself is unchanged, and each store decides finality from its own keys. \\
\code{file send-history} & Seal this file's event history (a KQHS snapshot, no content) to a label, its root signed by you. \\
\code{file open-history} & Open a history snapshot letter and compare it with your copy (\code{--against}). \\
\code{file request} & Ask a holder for a file (\code{--file-id} and \code{--name}), or with \code{--change} and \code{--message} to change one you hold. Writes a sealed request (\code{--output-dir}) or uploads it (\code{--push}). \\
\code{file open-request} & Open a request made of you; \code{--file} records it once in your copy. \\
\code{file answer-request} & Accept or decline a request (\code{--decision}); \code{--file} records the answer once. An answer delivers nothing. \\
\code{file open-answer} & Open the holder's answer; \code{--file} records it against the request your copy shows you made. \\
\code{file bridge-approve} & As a member of a private bridge that reaches from the author to the scope, approve a revision with the bridge (kept in this store only). \\
\code{file resolve} & As a conflict's reviewer, keep one side (\code{--keep left|right}) or supply the edited result (\code{--from}), signed as yours; \code{--reject} refuses a proposed merge. \\
\code{file merge} & Merge two divergent heads automatically when the edits allow it; otherwise record the conflict and who must review it, and on a \flag{--features tui} build in an interactive terminal open the review. A merge is a new revision that stays untrusted until it is signed. \\
\code{file review} & Show what each side of a fork changed, and who reviews a conflict. \flag{--interactive} needs a build with \flag{--features tui}. \\
\code{file graph} & Show the revisions, their parents and their trust. \\
\code{file diff} & Show the changed lines between two revisions of text. \\
\code{file checkout} & Write a revision's native bytes to a new file; \flag{--record} appends an event. \\
\code{file list} & List the tracked files this store has indexed (a cache; see \code{reindex}). \\
\code{file reindex} & Rebuild index rows from tracked files. Every file is verified first; if any fails, nothing changes. \flag{--clear} also drops rows for files not named here. \\
\code{file status} & Show the heads, their trust, what would be shared, and the current and latest trusted revision ids. \\
\code{file history} & List the recorded events; \flag{--export} also writes a \code{KQHS} snapshot. \\
\code{file history export} & List the events and write a \code{KQHS} snapshot to \flag{--out}. The one home for exporting; \code{file history --export} is legacy. \\
\code{file history verify} & Check a snapshot (with \flag{--against}, that it belongs to a file); given a tracked file it is \code{file verify}. The one home for verifying; \code{file verify-snapshot} is legacy. \\
\code{file verify-snapshot} & (Legacy; use \code{file history verify}.) Check a history snapshot; with \flag{--against}, that it belongs to a file. \\
\code{file import} & Bring another copy of the same file's revisions and proofs into this one. A fork is kept as two heads; nothing is overwritten. \\
\code{file verify} & Verify the history chain and revision graph, and judge every revision. \\
\code{file expire} & Schedule when this file's content is destroyed, or destroy it now; needs \flag{--slot} to prove you hold the owner label. Every retained revision's content goes at once; the history, the revision graph and every signature stay behind as a tombstone. \\
\code{file link} & Record what happens at a quorum-protected or password-locked file's gate in this tracked file's history. The gate is unchanged and never depends on it. \\
\code{file unlink} & Stop recording a gate in this tracked file's history. \\
\code{file share} & (Older; \code{send} runs it.) Seal the head (or \flag{--revision}) to another label as a \file{.kqpb} letter, or its most recently stored trusted ancestor when it is not trusted. Trusted work on another branch is never substituted, and nothing is sent when no ancestor is trusted. \\
\code{file receive} & (Older; \code{inbox open ID --into|--out} runs it. Still how a letter carried as a file is opened.) Open a tracked-file letter addressed to you. The sender is checked as the transport; the delivered revision is then judged by the file's own policy and accepted only if it is trusted here. Prints and records how the history compares with what this store accepted before (\code{FIRST}, \code{REPLAYED}, \code{NEWER}, \code{NOT_NEWER}); receiving the same letter again records nothing new. \\
\code{file ack} & (Older; \code{inbox open ID --file}.) Record an acknowledgement sealed back to you in your copy's history. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsection{\code{keyquorum-device}: the container tool}

A separate binary for one physical container of logical identity slots,
used without an organization database (see
\hyperref[sec:devices-and-custody]{Devices and custody}). Private keys
stay in the slot token; this tool never prints them.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Command} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\code{init} & Create \file{device.kq} and an empty vault directory. \\
\code{provision} & Add a slot; prints its public keys and prompts for a
  passphrase. \\
\code{list} & List slot labels and public keys from \file{device.kq}. \\
\code{public} & Print one slot's public keys. \\
\code{unwrap-share} & Unwrap a sealed share with a slot's encryption key;
  the raw share prints to stdout. \\
\code{sign} & Sign stdin with a slot's signing key; the hex signature
  prints to stdout. \\
\code{relocate} & Move a slot onto another container. The keypair is
  unchanged; only the device id it is bound to changes. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
