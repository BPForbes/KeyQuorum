\section{File formats quick reference}

Every on-disk and on-wire artifact this manual mentions has its own magic
bytes, so a store can tell at a glance what it is holding without trusting
a file extension.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{0.56\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Magic} & \textbf{Extension} & \textbf{What it is} \\
\midrule
\code{KQPB} & \file{.kqpb} & Sealed \code{PACKAGE} envelope: private-bridge
  letters, authenticated updates, device transfer/relocate letters, file
  delivery letters and acks, tracked-file letters and acks. \\
\code{KQXB} & \file{.kqxb} & Sealed \code{EXPORT_BUNDLE}: a portable
  credential or file export. \\
\code{KQBS} & \file{.kqbs} & Signature artifact from \code{sign} against a
  private bridge roster. \\
\code{KQDV} & \file{device.kq} & Signed device descriptor, verified against
  \file{device.skey}. \\
\code{KQST} & \file{*.kqst} & A slot token, sealing one logical identity to
  a device id. \\
\code{KQTX} & --- & Device transfer package, signed by the source device,
  bound to a destination device id. Never stored raw on the mailbox. \\
\code{KQTF} & \file{.kqtf} & Tracked file: revisions, proofs, policy and
  hash-chained history (version 5; a payload may be absent after expiry;
  version 4 still opens). Not a sealed envelope. \\
\code{KQHS} & \file{.kqhs} & History snapshot: a tracked file's event
  history alone, verifiable on its own and against its file. \\
--- & \file{.kqbn} & Eviction notice --- a public routing slip, not a sealed
  envelope. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{noteBox}
\code{KQPB} and \code{KQXB} share one sealed-envelope framing (magic,
version, kind byte, recipient X25519 public key, sealed length); they are
two \emph{formats} of the same codec, not two independent designs. Device
copy, move, and relocate letters are additional \code{PACKAGE} kind bytes
under \code{KQPB}, not a separate format.
\end{noteBox}

\subsection{Roadmap}

Private-key custody today is a key file (one key, one device) or a
\code{keyquorum-device} container (one directory, many passphrase-wrapped
slots). \code{generate} still prints a private key once and does not write
it. Real USB token protocols are not implemented; a container is a
directory, not an OS partition.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Persisted private-key custody} for \code{generate} beyond
  the key file and the slot token (for example, an OS keychain).
  \item \textbf{\code{import}} of password-vault / locked-file
  \code{export} bundles. The bundle format and encoder are already final;
  private-bridge \file{.kqpb} import is already implemented.
\end{itemize}
